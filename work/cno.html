CNO · Healthcare Communication, Freelance

Editorial care for a healthcare brand

A running retainer for a pediatric orthodontic practice, balancing clinical trust with a social presence people actually follow.

Client: Centro Napoletano Ortodonzia (CNO), Naples
Role: Freelance Social Media Strategist
Timeline: Feb 2025 – Dec 2025
Channels: Instagram, Facebook (cross-posted)

One of the educational posts I designed to make orthodontic care approachable for parents and kids, without the clinical tone.

Overview

CNO is a healthcare client, an orthodontic practice in Naples specialising in orthodontics for children and teenagers, and one of the two clients I ran as sole point of contact since going freelance: onboarding, briefing, contract scope and deadlines, start to finish.

The Challenge

Healthcare content has to earn trust before it earns attention. It can't be as loud as retail content, but it still has to work on Instagram, where quiet and forgettable are the same thing. The brief was to be credible without being clinical, and visible without overselling. Parents booking orthodontic care for their kids also don't respond well to technical, clinical language, which is a common mistake in healthcare communication.

My Role

End-to-end: strategy, content calendar, creation and publishing, with no supervision and no one else to catch what I might have missed. The same setup I had with my other freelance client at the time.

Process

  • Built and maintained the editorial calendar, planning content around the practice's real patient journey rather than generic dental content.
  • Went deeper on buyer personas than I had at Goeldlin, to write content that actually spoke to parents and young patients, not just at them.
  • Kept the language simple and non-technical throughout, on purpose, since over-clinical language is where a lot of healthcare content loses people.
  • Organised the workload independently, alongside another concurrent client and their own deadlines.

Where it stands

The retainer ran continuously from February to December 2025. For a healthcare client, that continuity was itself the outcome: it meant the content did its quieter job of keeping the practice visible and trusted. By May 2025, monthly views on Reels and posts had reached 7,911 (+957% on the previous month), with 47% of those views coming from non-followers, a sign the content was reaching beyond the existing audience rather than just recycling it. The account had grown to 633 followers by the same point, modest but steady growth for a healthcare practice where trust, not virality, is the goal.

Social graphics

A wider selection of the content I designed for CNO: educational carousels, prevention campaigns, patient-facing promos and awareness days, all kept approachable for parents of young children, my main audience, rather than clinical.
